Your Qidi Q2, on open software

Mainline Klipper, Happy Hare and Spoolman run on a computer next to your printer. Tag a spool, put it in the Box, and the printer takes it from there.

Install Follow a spool

Scroll to follow a new spool from the bag to the first layer

1

A new spool

Any brand. It doesn't need a chip from the manufacturer or a reader of its own.

2

Stick an NFC tag on it

An NTAG215 sticker on the flange, on the side that faces the neighbouring gate. Leave it unlocked.

The tags cost a few cents each and can be rewritten.

3

Write it from your phone

An OpenSpool app writes brand, material, colour and temperatures to the tag. It's an open format, read by other printers too.

Spell brand and material the same way each time, so Spoolman doesn't create duplicates.

4

Put it in the Box

Any of the four gates. When the Box preloads the spool, its NFC reader picks up the tag.

5

The printer knows the spool

Happy Hare creates the spool in Spoolman on the first scan. When brand, material and colour match SpoolmanDB, it gets the manufacturer's name, temperatures, density and spool weight.

Later scans of the same tag select that spool again, in whatever gate you put it.

6

It asks before it measures

For a filament it has never measured, the touchscreen offers to calibrate pressure advance now, at the next print that uses it, or not at all.

7

The load cell measures it

At the temperature and flow ratio your slicer prints that filament with, the nozzle tries a range of pressure advance values over the purge chute and the load cell picks the best one. Then it wipes the nozzle.

The sweep is autopa's. Each step shows on the touchscreen, Mainsail and Fluidd.

8

Saved once, used everywhere

The value is stored on the filament in Spoolman, and the OrcaSlicer presets pick it up within a minute. Your next spool of the same filament reuses it.

9

Print

OrcaSlicer sees what's in each gate and selects the preset of that exact spool. You slice, send, and the printer loads it.

Everything else is set up too

The installer configures the Q2 and the Box completely. You don't start from an empty printer.cfg.

  • Mainline Klipper

    Current Klipper and Moonraker on a mini PC, a NAS or a Raspberry Pi 4/5. The printer's own board only forwards the three MCU links, runs the touchscreen and the camera.

  • Load cell probing

    Bed probing with the toolhead load cell, and a bed mesh that only covers the part you print.

  • Drying in the Box

    Drying from the touchscreen, a keep-dry watchdog afterwards, and spool rotation so the filament dries evenly.

  • Help when a load fails

    When a spool doesn't load, a dialog on the touchscreen offers to retry or to recover it.

  • Power-loss recovery

    The print state is saved as it goes, so a print stopped by a power cut can resume.

  • Failure detection

    Obico's spaghetti detector watches the camera and can pause a failing print. It runs on your host, not in a cloud.

  • Updates in one click

    From the update manager in Mainsail or Fluidd, on the stable channel (tested on a printer) or the latest one.

  • Works without a Box

    Install with --no-box: the toolhead sensor pauses the print when filament runs out.

Two machines, one printer

Klipper needs more than the printer's board can give. q2-offload moves the heavy work to a computer on your network and leaves the board the jobs it's good at.

Install

On the Klipper host, as your normal user. The installer shows a menu of the optional components and asks before each step that touches the printer.

git clone https://github.com/cedbossneo/q2-offload.git ~/q2-offload
cd ~/q2-offload
# latest stable version
git checkout "$(git tag -l 'v*' --sort=-creatordate | grep -v -- -beta | head -1)"
./install.sh --printer 192.168.1.50

What you need

  • A Qidi Q2 with one Qidi Box, on the stock Qidi OS, reachable over SSH. Or no Box, with --no-box.
  • A host on Debian 12 or 13, or Ubuntu 22.04, 24.04 or 26.04 LTS, x86_64 or arm64. CI installs on each of them.
  • Both on the same network, wired if possible.

Read this before you start

It replaces Qidi's firmware on three microcontrollers. The first conversion replaces Qidi's bootloader with Katapult: a power cut during that step, or a wrong image, can only be recovered with an ST-Link. Converting from stock firmware 01.01.02 or newer isn't tested yet. Read the recovery guide first. No warranty: you do this at your own risk.
