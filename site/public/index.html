Your Qidi Q2, on open software

Mainline Klipper, Happy Hare and Spoolman run on a computer next to your printer. Tag a spool, put it in the Box, and the printer takes it from there.

Install See how it works

Scroll: setting it up, a new spool, and every day

1

A computer next to the printer

A mini PC, a NAS or a Raspberry Pi 4/5 on Debian or Ubuntu, wired to the same network as the printer. That's where Klipper will run.

Debian 12 and 13, Ubuntu 22.04, 24.04 and 26.04, x86_64 and arm64: CI installs on each of them.

2

One installer

It installs Klipper, Moonraker, Happy Hare and the components you pick from its menu, then asks before every step that touches the printer.

3

Katapult, without an ST‑Link

After a backup of the printer config, Qidi's own update scripts put the Katapult bootloader on the mainboard, the toolhead and the Box.

This first conversion is the one risky step: a power cut during it can only be recovered with an ST‑Link.

4

Klipper on all three MCUs

It checks each MCU's application offset before writing, then flashes Klipper through Katapult. Later updates reflash the same way, after a typed confirmation.

5

The printer's board becomes a bridge

Its stock Klipper and screen are stopped. It forwards the three MCU links over the network, and runs HelixScreen on the touchscreen, connected to the host.

If the printer or the host restarts, a watchdog reconnects Klipper on its own.

1

A new spool

Any brand. It doesn't need a chip from the manufacturer or a reader of its own.

2

Stick an NFC tag on it

An NTAG215 sticker on the flange, on the side that faces the neighbouring gate. Leave it unlocked.

The tags cost a few cents each and can be rewritten.

3

Write it from your phone

An OpenSpool app writes brand, material, colour and temperatures to the tag. It's an open format, read by other printers too.

Spell brand and material the same way each time, so Spoolman doesn't create duplicates.

4

Put it in the Box

Any of the four gates. When the Box preloads the spool, its NFC reader picks up the tag.

5

The printer knows the spool

Happy Hare creates the spool in Spoolman on the first scan. When brand, material and colour match SpoolmanDB, it gets the manufacturer's name, temperatures, density and spool weight.

Later scans of the same tag select that spool again, in whatever gate you put it.

6

It asks before it measures

For a filament it has never measured, the touchscreen offers to calibrate pressure advance now, at the next print that uses it, or not at all.

7

The load cell measures it

At the temperature and flow ratio your slicer prints that filament with, the nozzle tries a range of pressure advance values over the purge chute and the load cell picks the best one. Then it wipes the nozzle.

The sweep is autopa's. Each step shows on the touchscreen, Mainsail and Fluidd.

8

Saved once, used everywhere

The value is stored on the filament in Spoolman, and the OrcaSlicer presets pick it up within a minute. Your next spool of the same filament reuses it.

9

Print

OrcaSlicer sees what's in each gate and selects the preset of that exact spool. You slice, send, and the printer loads it.

1

Dry filament in the Box

Start a cycle from the Box page or the touchscreen: each material has its temperature and time, and the time left shows as it runs. Afterwards, keep-dry watches the humidity.

With the spools out, the Box turns each empty gate every 5 minutes so it heats evenly, like the stock firmware.

2

Happy Hare runs the Box

It unloads one gate and loads another for a colour change, keeps the gate map in sync with Spoolman, and tracks what is left on each spool.

3

HelixScreen on the printer

The touchscreen runs HelixScreen, an open Klipper interface, talking to the host. It shows the Box, the drying, and the dialogs q2-offload opens.

4

A camera watches every print

Obico's spaghetti detector looks at a frame every 10 seconds, on your host. It warns you, and can pause the print and send you the picture.

Every decision is kept with its picture, so you can see how reliable it is on your printer.

Made here, for the Q2

The parts that didn't exist anywhere else. All of it is in the repository, and the same installer sets it up.

  • The installer and updates

    Converts a stock printer, then updates from Mainsail or Fluidd on the stable channel (tested on a printer) or the latest one.

  • The network bridge

    Serial links over TCP to the printer's board, and a watchdog that reconnects Klipper after a restart or a dropped link.

  • Q2 configuration and Klipper patches

    Load cell probing, an adaptive bed mesh, purge bucket and chute macros, power-loss recovery, and Klipper patches such as board fan temperature limits and a load cell timing fix.

  • NFC spools and pressure advance

    SpoolmanDB data for any brand, and a load cell calibration offered for each new filament, saved in Spoolman.

  • Box drying page

    Humidity, temperature and loaded spools, a drying cycle with its time left, keep-dry settings, and spool rotation with the spools out.

  • Failure detection service

    Runs Obico's detector on your host with its alert rules, a live page, a history of every print, and notifications through Apprise.

  • Q2 Spool Presets for OrcaSlicer

    A Box tab in the slicer, with one preset per Spoolman spool, its flow ratio and pressure advance.

  • Happy Hare for the Qidi Box

    Its configuration for the Box, a retry dialog for failed loads, and patches proposed upstream to Happy Hare.

Built on open source

Everything that runs your printer is free software you can read, change and keep.

  • Klipper

    The firmware, running on the host and the three MCUs.

  • Moonraker

    The API that the web interfaces, the touchscreen and the slicer talk to.

  • Katapult

    The bootloader that lets the MCUs be reflashed over their links.

  • Happy Hare

    Runs the Qidi Box: gates, loads, NFC tags, drying.

  • Spoolman

    Keeps every spool and filament, with SpoolmanDB's manufacturer data.

  • HelixScreen

    The touchscreen interface.

  • Mainsail and Fluidd

    The web interfaces.

  • autopa

    The load cell pressure advance sweep.

  • Obico

    The spaghetti detector and its alert rules.

  • OrcaSlicer

    The slicer, with q2-offload's presets and plugin.

  • OpenSpool

    The open NFC tag format for spools.

  • Apprise

    Notifications to your phone, Telegram, Discord or Home Assistant.

And the Q2 community work it started from: MisterSheikh's Klipper patches for the Q2 MCUs, n3oney's Katapult deployers that make the conversion possible without an ST-Link, the serial-over-network design of endeavour and theboleslaw, and Qidi's own published configuration.

Two machines, one printer

Klipper needs more than the printer's board can give. q2-offload moves the heavy work to a computer on your network and leaves the board the jobs it's good at.

Install

On the Klipper host, as your normal user. The installer shows a menu of the optional components and asks before each step that touches the printer.

git clone https://github.com/cedbossneo/q2-offload.git ~/q2-offload
cd ~/q2-offload
# latest stable version
git checkout "$(git tag -l 'v*' --sort=-creatordate | grep -v -- -beta | head -1)"
./install.sh --printer 192.168.1.50

What you need

  • A Qidi Q2 with one Qidi Box, on the stock Qidi OS, reachable over SSH. Or no Box, with --no-box.
  • A host on Debian 12 or 13, or Ubuntu 22.04, 24.04 or 26.04 LTS, x86_64 or arm64. CI installs on each of them.
  • Both on the same network, wired if possible.
Read the install guide

Read this before you start

It replaces Qidi's firmware on three microcontrollers. The first conversion replaces Qidi's bootloader with Katapult: a power cut during that step, or a wrong image, can only be recovered with an ST-Link. Converting from stock firmware 01.01.02 or newer isn't tested yet. Read Before you start first. No warranty: you do this at your own risk.
